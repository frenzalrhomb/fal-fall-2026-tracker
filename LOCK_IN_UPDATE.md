## Decision method

The objective is high expected team points under the roster, restricted-title and swap constraints. It is not personal viewing enjoyment. It is also not a modeled probability of winning first place: opponent ownership, rank distributions and a validated joint outcome model are unavailable.

I used the same evidence hierarchy across candidates:
1. Exact roster eligibility and restricted-slot opportunity cost.
2. Actual post-premiere MAL Watching + Completed where meaningful; otherwise MAL audience scale and sustained growth.
3. Reception evidence relevant to future scores, drops, favorites and discussions. Current prequel/source AniList scores are comparative priors, not converted MAL predictions.
4. Confirmed US/international availability and time to build an audience before scoring.
5. Independent AniList, poll and YouTube corroboration. Correlated popularity signals are not separate independent votes.

No learned platform penalty, unofficial-viewing percentage, hand-picked numeric hype weights, precise season-point totals or win probabilities are asserted. The project is an evidence tracker with rule arithmetic, not a trained, historically validated season forecast. A pure algorithmic optimum is not established by these data.

## Why each active title is selected

### Reincarnated as a Sword S2 — Tensei shitara Ken deshita II

96,604 MAL members and 38,879 AniList popularity lead the eligible field. Its latest MAL gain is +541. This is the strongest audience-volume anchor. Current prior-anime reception is 74/100 on AniList, so the case is not that it will have the highest rating.

HIDIVE is its confirmed US route; lack of Crunchyroll/Netflix is not a veto. Its established demand is considerably larger than the other restricted contenders, and advance streaming makes it available before October 4. Audience conversion remains uncertain, but it would take a substantial shortfall to discard that lead.

### Blue Box S2 — Ao no Hako Season 2

76,210 MAL members, 35,172 AniList popularity, +689 latest MAL growth, an 81/100 prior anime and #3 / 6.3% in Anime Corner's anticipation poll. It combines audience size with a stronger reception prior than Sword.

Netflix's weekly October 4 release gives less time to accumulate Week 1 viewers, but this is a likely long-term starter. Benching it for a small possible Week 1 gain would use a scarce swap to bring it back.

### The Ramparts of Ice S2 — Koori no Jouheki 2nd Season

40,426 MAL members, +669, and 21,548 AniList popularity. The previous anime is 83/100 and manga 85/100 on AniList. Poll support is #5 / 4.3%. These are particularly good signals for the combination of audience and reception that FAL rewards.

Its tracked second PV increased from 694,845 to 832,122 views (+137,277); the audience listing growth is the more relevant corroboration. Netflix explicitly announces worldwide weekly streaming from October 2, before Week 1.

### Appraisal Skill S3 — Tensei Kizoku, Kantei Skill de Nariagaru 3rd Season

41,202 MAL members, +495, and 15,099 AniList popularity. The preceding anime's AniList score is 73/100. Its large established audience and September 27 Crunchyroll release provide a useful early scoring opportunity.

This is a medium-confidence starter, not an untouchable selection. Firefly or Ranma can replace it later if their actual total FAL contribution is better. Its current snapshot predates the announced premiere, so pre-airing status counts are not evidence of failed conversion.

### A Wild Last Boss Appeared! S2 — Yasei no Last Boss ga Arawareta! 2nd Season

This remains in the eight as an upside option, but the post-premiere evidence does not by itself establish a season-long advantage:
- MAL members: 19,559 → 23,400, **+3,841 / +19.6%** in approximately 25 hours.
- MAL Watching + Completed: 176 → **6,047**.
- MAL score: **7.60 from 924 ratings**.
- MAL dropped: **32**; too early to call this a stable retention rate.
- AniList popularity: 10,688 → **12,869**, +2,181.

That is a real first-week advantage, but a single early audience snapshot is small relative to 13 weeks of ratings, retention, favorites, discussions and future audience growth. I put it on the bench and start Ranma: Ranma has a larger MAL base (26,786 vs 23,400), a stronger prior-anime AniList score (77 vs 74), and confirmed worldwide access. Both should be released before the Week 1 cutoff. Compare actual post-release data after Wild Last Boss's second episode and the other titles' premieres; promote it only if its continued viewing and reception justify one of the four swaps.

## Why Seitokai is out

**Even the Student Council Has Its Holes! / Seitokai ni mo Ana wa Aru!** has 24,838 MAL members (+755), 14,531 AniList popularity (+387), and two million-view PVs. This is real interest from international tracking-site users, not just an inference from Japanese trailer comments.

However, the official publisher's listed streaming platforms are domestic Japanese availability. Netflix Japan listing does not establish Netflix US rights. I could not verify a US release at this check. That means uncertain availability, not confirmed absence for the entire season or zero international viewers.

MAL is a global community. Every qualifying MAL list status counts, regardless of country or service. We do not know the percentage who would watch via unofficial sources. I therefore do not assign an invented 30%, 50% or other audience haircut.

I leave it outside this roster because:
- Wild Last Boss has promising early adoption, but that single post-premiere snapshot is not directly comparable to unreleased titles.
- Firefly has a much larger MAL audience and substantially stronger anticipation-poll support.
- Ranma has an established anime reception prior and confirmed worldwide distribution.
- Returner has nearly twice its MAL audience and a larger AniList audience.

Seitokai's faster growth could overcome these disadvantages. Its exclusion is a genuine forecast risk, and would look too conservative if it rapidly gains accessible English distribution and a highly engaged audience. The evidence does not support treating lack of a US announcement as an automatic disqualification.

## FAL arithmetic and operating strategy

Audience scoring is **Watching + Completed after airing**, not total members or Plan to Watch. Read the supplied extra +0.25 on even weeks as an addition to the regular +0.5. That is 0.5 normally and 0.75 in Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12.

Illustrative marginal effects, assuming a title is active at all relevant cutoffs:
- 10,000 extra Watching + Completed at every one of the 13 cutoffs: **80,000 points**.
- 0.5 higher MAL rating at each score cutoff: **43,750–52,500 points**, depending on whether the Week 13 coefficient replaces or stacks with the ordinary coefficient.
- 1,000 more dropped users at each dropped-user checkpoint: **20,000–24,000 points lost** under those two Week 13 readings.
- 1,000 additional MAL favorites at each favorites checkpoint: **75,000–90,000 points**.
- 100 additional unique participants summed per eligible episode thread at the ordinary discussion rate: **7,500 points**.

These are sensitivities, not title forecasts. The live rules page could not be opened; calculations use the user's supplied rules. Its Week 13 wording has not been reconciled against an official worked example, so no exact 13-week projections are asserted. The existing scoring.py also has a global replacement-mode interpretation of the even-week bonus; this report explicitly uses the additive audience reading and does not rely on that alternative as the baseline.

The consequences for this roster:
1. **Start Sword, Blue Box, Ramparts, Appraisal and Ranma.** They all have scheduled releases before Week 1, October 4 at 22:00 UTC.
2. **Do not choose an Ace from preseason total members.** Before the Week 1 cutoff, use actual/forthcoming Watching + Completed and the 60,000 eligibility threshold. Sword and Wild Last Boss warrant attention; neither is declared a guaranteed winner now. One Ace per title, one per week; successful +75,000, failure/ineligibility -5,000.
3. **Review Firefly after October 9.** It is the first likely challenger for an active slot. Compare total expected upcoming points against all five, especially Appraisal and Wild Last Boss. Do not precommit to removing either.
4. **Reassess Wild Last Boss after its second episode and at the first score checkpoint, October 18.** Compare its updated Watching + Completed, score and dropped count against Ranma and the other active titles. Promote it only if its projected upcoming points justify a swap. Returner enters only if its audience advantage compensates for its weaker reception outlook.
5. **Protect the four-swap budget.** Only one regular swap per week. One-way sustained upgrades are preferable to repeatedly rotating for small one-week gains.
6. **Do not bench a completed anime automatically.** Completed users still contribute to the recurring audience total.
7. **Week 10 wildcard:** Booster gives +10,000. An extra swap costs 5,000, so it needs more than 15,000 additional title points to beat Booster. Bombers reduce our own points and are only relevant to a specific rank situation.
8. Outside alternatives cannot be added after registration. Bench membership preserves an option; it does not score until the title is active, apart from the stated tie-break use.

A strong roster alone cannot guarantee the best 13-week result. Aces and four swaps are material decisions that require in-season observations. There is no defensible fixed preseason schedule that guarantees all those actions succeed.
