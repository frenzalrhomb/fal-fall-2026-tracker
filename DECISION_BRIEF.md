## Why each starter is selected

### 1. Reincarnated as a Sword S2 — audience anchor

At **96,063 MAL members** and **38,567 AniList popularity**, Sword leads the eligible field on both measures. That is more than double Appraisal or Ramparts on MAL. Its +410 latest MAL gain is healthy, although the percentage growth is modest because the starting base is large.

The main FAL case is recurring Watching + Completed points. The preceding anime's current AniList reception is **74/100**, which supports an established audience but does not justify expecting elite MAL scores. It does not need to win every quality checkpoint to be useful.

**Why active:** advance distribution begins before October 4. Starting it avoids spending a limited swap to introduce a likely long-term scorer.

**Risk:** Plan to Watch conversion and streaming reach. Its 96,063 total members do **not** make it Ace-ineligible; only Watching + Completed matters for the 60,000 threshold.

**Confidence in inclusion: high.**

### 2. Blue Box S2 — audience plus stronger reception prior

Blue Box combines **75,521 MAL members**, **34,899 AniList popularity**, +551 latest MAL growth, and **#3 / 6.3%** in Anime Corner's poll. It is the highest-ranked poll title in our eligible pool. The preceding anime's current AniList score is **81/100**.

This is the clearest selected candidate for a combination of large viewing totals and favorable score/favorites weeks. Those are expectations from existing reception, not forecasts that MAL will assign a particular score.

The tracked Netflix English-market main trailer has **754,176 views**, +5,808 over the latest interval. The relevant comment sample was 93% English out of 100 threads; that establishes English-language engagement in this sample, not the nationality of every viewer.

**Why active:** [Netflix says October 4](https://media.netflix.com/en/only-on-netflix/81663323), before the first scoring cutoff on the current schedule. It has less accumulation time in week one than Appraisal or Ramparts, but belongs in the long-term five.

**Risk:** day-one viewing conversion and actual episode availability relative to the cutoff.

**Confidence: high.**

### 3. The Ramparts of Ice S2 — strongest unrestricted all-round case

Ramparts has **39,757 MAL members**, **21,232 AniList popularity**, +527 / +277 latest gains, and poll **#5 / 4.3%**. MAL membership is up **19.5% since September 14**.

The preceding anime is currently **83/100 on AniList** and the source manga **85/100**. These are among our stronger reception priors. This is why I prefer Ramparts to simply picking the biggest remaining sequel: it offers a credible route to good score-week points as well as growing audience.

The latest +105,842 views on its second PV reinforces the attention signal. The sample is still heavily Japanese, so the MAL and AniList movement matters more than the raw view spike.

**Why active:** [official TV start October 1; Netflix from October 2](https://korinojoheki-pr.com/), both before week one.

**Risk:** promotional exposure may convert weakly outside Japan; second-season quality remains unknown.

**Confidence: high.**

### 4. Appraisal Skill S3 — useful established starter, not untouchable

Its **40,707 MAL members** and **14,773 AniList popularity** give it a substantial audience ahead of the smaller breakout candidates. It added +356 MAL / +182 AniList over the latest interval. The previous anime's current AniList score is **73/100**.

It is selected for a reasonable established audience plus an early start. I am not selecting it just to win week one: it still has a larger current MAL base than Firefly, Seitokai, Dragon Ball, Ranma or Love-Potion. Its confirmed English dub is additional availability support.

**Why active:** [premiere around September 27/28](https://kanteiskill.com/onair/), giving the audience time to accumulate before October 4.

**Risk:** slower growth and a weaker reception prior than Blue Box/Ramparts. If Firefly or Seitokai substantially outperform after release, Appraisal is a plausible outgoing active title.

**Confidence: medium.**

### 5. Seitokai ni mo Ana wa Aru! — measured breakout bet

At **24,083 MAL members** it is smaller, but +650 latest MAL growth is second in the entire field. Its **14,144 AniList popularity** is far closer to Appraisal's 14,773 than the MAL counts alone imply. It is up **40.7% on MAL since September 14**.

Two tracked PVs have each exceeded one million views; PV2 gained another 43,474. This is not just an isolated large lifetime view count: multiple days of actual listing growth accompany the publicity.

Its source manga's current AniList score is **75/100**, so I do not assume an exceptional eventual MAL rating. The case is a rapidly building audience with upside for discussion activity. Unique MAL discussion users are not yet measured.

**Why active:** [October 3 at 24:30 JST / October 4 civil date](https://www.aniplex.co.jp/lineup/nama-anaru/) falls before Week 1. Domestic broader streaming is listed later; the international schedule is less firmly verified than Blue Box's, adding conversion risk.

**Risk:** niche comedy reception, weaker poll result (**#24 / 0.9%**) and Japanese-heavy promotion. It is the least certain initial starter.

**Why over Love-Potion:** stronger current MAL base, much larger AniList base (**14,144 vs 8,619**), larger latest gains, and a pre-week-one premiere.

**Confidence: medium; upside selection.**

## Why each bench title is selected

### 6. Firefly Wedding — highest-priority potential swap-in

**36,509 MAL members**, **19,426 AniList popularity**, +671 MAL / +282 AniList, **#6 / 3.3%** in the poll, and a confirmed Crunchyroll English dub create the strongest new-adaptation case in the roster. MAL membership has grown **26.1% since September 14**.

Its source manga is **78/100 on AniList**. This supports a favorable reception prior, while adaptation execution remains uncertain. I expect it to compete for a long-term active place if its audience converts.

**Why bench:** the [official premiere is October 9](https://hotaru-anime.com/). It misses Week 1. Bench placement is entirely a timing choice, not a claim that Appraisal or Seitokai is a stronger season-long selection.

**Risk:** a successful manga does not guarantee a successful adaptation; premiere momentum could fade.

**Confidence in the eight: high.**

### 7. A Returner's Magic Should Be Special S2 — established audience insurance

Returner has **46,431 MAL members** and **20,518 AniList popularity**. That is a much bigger already-expressed audience than Love-Potion or Dragon Ball. Latest growth of +288 MAL / +150 AniList is modest but positive; the confirmed dub and broad Crunchyroll availability help the viewing path.

The weak point is reception: the previous anime is **69/100 on AniList**. I am not assuming it scores like Ramparts. It earns its place as a sizeable sequel whose actual conversion can be inspected before committing a swap.

**Why bench:** Crunchyroll lists October 7 and MAL's Japanese date is October 8; both fall after Week 1.

**Risk:** old Plan to Watch additions may be inactive interest; weak ratings and drops can overwhelm an audience advantage. It is insurance, not an automatic week-three starter.

**Why over Detective:** Detective has an 11,547-member MAL advantage, but their AniList audiences are almost equal (20,615 vs 20,518), their latest gains are similar, and Detective's previous-anime reception is considerably weaker at 61/100.

**Confidence: medium.**

### 8. Dragon Ball Super: Beerus — final slot, strongest international upside case

This is the closest selection. It has **28,868 MAL members**, **11,205 AniList popularity**, +320 MAL / +149 AniList, and poll **#8 / 3.1%**.

The tracked official trailer has **5,176,957 views**, +26,708 over the latest interval. Despite appearing on a Japanese channel, its 100-thread relevant sample was **83% English**, and the recent sample **68% English**. This is meaningful evidence of international attention; it is not a measurement of Western viewers or MAL conversion.

The [official site describes an enhanced reconstruction with added and redrawn material](https://dragonball-super.com/). That gives an upside argument, but existing fans may skip an entry revisiting familiar material.

**Why bench:** official start October 11, after Week 1 and only hours before Week 2 scoring. It need not enter the active five immediately. The other five starters protect us while we observe whether the franchise attention converts.

**Why narrowly over Love-Potion:** 37% larger current MAL audience, 30% larger AniList audience, roughly three times the poll share, and stronger measured English-language trailer engagement. Love-Potion wins on recent growth; Dragon Ball wins the broader existing-demand comparison.

**Risk:** low MAL adoption for reworked material, a late start, uncertain new-edition dub and no guarantee of a useful completion spike before the league ends. This is the most replaceable slot before registration.

**Confidence: medium-low.**

## Why the leading alternatives did not make the eight

### Love-Potion Witch — closest momentum challenger

**21,053 MAL; +555 latest interval; 8,619 AniList; +284.** MAL membership is up **43.5% since September 14**. Its main PV has 627,386 views, +28,083.

There is real momentum. It also has [confirmed Crunchyroll subtitled distribution from October 5](https://www.crunchyroll.com/id/news/seasonal-lineup/2026/9/15/fall-anime-2026-crunchyroll), so it would be wrong to claim no route to an international audience. An English dub is unconfirmed.

It narrowly misses because it still trails Seitokai across audience and current growth, and trails Dragon Ball across audience size and poll demand. Its main PV's relevant sample is **92% Japanese / 8% English**, while its recent sample is **58% Japanese / 26% English**; the sampling method affects the picture. These are bounded samples, not the show's overall demographics. X mention/engagement growth is not measured.

I would choose it **instead of Dragon Ball** only for a deliberate bet on a new romance adaptation outperforming established international franchise demand. My single lock-in recommendation keeps Dragon Ball.

### The Detective Is Already Dead S2 — strong raw size, weaker quality prior

**57,978 MAL; +248; 20,615 AniList; poll #10 / 2.8%; English dub confirmed.** It is a real threat to beat our selection.

The previous anime's **61/100 AniList** score, compared with Returner's 69/100 and Ramparts' 83/100, raises conversion, rating and drop concerns. These are comparative priors, not predictions that Season 2 must receive the same scores. Most of its MAL lead over Returner is not reproduced on AniList.

Since FAL pays for scores and subtracts repeated drop snapshots, I am unwilling to select it solely on its Plan to Watch base. Its premiere also misses Week 1.

### Ranma 1/2 S3 — closest steadier alternative

**26,439 MAL; +256; 10,824 AniList; poll #12 / 1.7%; previous anime 77/100 on AniList.** It has a sensible balanced case and a week-one start.

It loses narrowly to Seitokai's much faster listing growth and larger AniList audience; it loses to Dragon Ball on poll demand and current international engagement. If prioritizing a more established quality prior over upside, Ranma is a reasonable alternative to Dragon Ball, but it is not my selected eight.

### A Wild Last Boss Appeared! S2 — too little post-premiere evidence

**19,559 MAL; +503; 10,688 AniList; poll #20 / 1.1%; previous anime 74/100 on AniList.** Its [September 26 advance premiere](https://www.lastboss-anime.com/news/index00320000.html) offers a useful head start.

The new 176 Watching + Completed count was sampled about 16 minutes after the scheduled premiere. Calling that strong or weak reception would be premature. Its current base is smaller than the selected sequels, and its tracked Crunchyroll trailer added only 548 views over the interval.

An early start alone does not make it a higher-value 13-week pick. I keep the larger audience and stronger cross-source selections.

It is a credible sleeper, but its MAL base is under half Dragon Ball's and less than a third Returner's. It would require substantial release-driven adoption to justify the slot.

### Why the other restricted titles lose

**Tokyo Revengers:** 62,283 MAL, +384; 24,208 AniList; poll #11 / 2.6%; prior anime 77/100. A credible alternative, but Blue Box beats it in MAL size, current growth, AniList and poll support. Sword has a very large audience lead. No third restricted slot is available.

**Ao Ashi:** 34,218 MAL, +249; 16,358 AniList; poll #17 / 1.3%; prior anime 81/100 and manga 87/100. Quality prior is good, but its current audience is far below both selected restricted titles. I prefer paying an unrestricted slot for Ramparts' quality rather than giving up Sword/Blue Box's audience.

## How this team earns FAL points

The roster balances audience anchors (Sword, Blue Box), stronger reception priors (Blue Box, Ramparts), measured breakout growth (Firefly, Seitokai), an early established sequel (Appraisal), audience insurance (Returner), and international upside (Dragon Ball).

- Audience points use **Watching + Completed after airing**, not Plan to Watch, total members, YouTube views or AniList counts.
- A 0.5-point MAL rating advantage at each score checkpoint (weeks 3, 7, 10, 13) is worth **43,750** across the season if the week-13 35,000 coefficient replaces the normal rate; **52,500** if it stacks. A title must actually be active at those checkpoints to earn that advantage.
- At the ordinary 75-point discussion rate, 100 more unique participants summed per eligible episode thread means **7,500** points. Reddit/YouTube comments do not substitute for that count.
- Completed users continue to score. A finale is not a reason to bench a show automatically.
- Favorites, drops and week-13 multipliers can reverse an audience-only ranking.

The copied rules' special even-week and week-13 wording has not been reconciled against an accessible official breakdown. Both coefficient readings exist in scoring.py; the examples above state the assumed reading. I could not freshly load the FAL rules page. No title-specific season point totals or win probabilities are fabricated.

## Practical plan after registration

1. **Start with the five shown above.** No deliberate zero-scoring bench title in Week 1 on the current schedule. Registration is for exactly eight titles; the outside alternatives cannot be added later.
2. **Week 1 Ace (before October 4, 22:00 UTC):** with audience as the only ordinary title component this week, inspect current Watching + Completed for the active five and forecast who will lead at cutoff while remaining within the 60,000 threshold. Sword is a candidate, not a predeclared winner. Do not set an Ace now solely on Plan to Watch.
3. **Week 2:** Firefly is the first bench title to evaluate. Make the one permitted swap only if its current and expected following-week points justify it. Appraisal/Seitokai are likely comparisons, but compare all five. Do not force a swap just because Firefly premieres.
4. **Week 3 (October 18):** the first score bonus matters. Returner may merit entry if audience conversion offsets lower rating expectations. Dragon Ball gets a slot only if actual conversion confirms the franchise bet.
5. **Protect the four-swap budget.** Do not precommit to bringing in all three bench titles. Keeping roughly two swaps available for material late-season changes is a useful default, not a restriction if an earlier swap has clearly greater value. Revisit weeks 7 (November 15), 10 (December 6), and 13 (December 27).
6. **Aces:** +75,000 for success, -5,000 for failure/ineligibility; each anime can be Aced once. The rules allow skipping a higher scorer that has crossed the Watching + Completed threshold. Do not assume merely having used that title's Ace removes it from competing for weekly leader. Apply the site's exact eligibility/tie treatment before declaring.
7. **Wildcard from Week 10:** Booster is +10,000. An extra swap costs 5,000, so its additional anime points must exceed 15,000 to beat Booster. Bombers lower our own total and should be considered only for a specific rank objective.

## Evidence limits

Latest successful coverage is MAL **69/69**, AniList **63/69** (all eight picks and named main challengers covered), and YouTube **84/84 registered videos**. There are 13 UTC observation dates from September 14–26. The YouTube registry is a selected set, not every upload. Reddit is paused after access denial; X and Google Trends are not measured. MAL season scores were still unavailable in the snapshot; MAL favorites and unique episode-discussion users are not yet collected. Source/prequel AniList ratings are current reception priors, not direct conversions into MAL forecast scores.

This is sufficient to make an informed roster decision, but not to claim a historically validated optimum. My strongest inclusion convictions are Sword, Blue Box, Ramparts and Firefly; the most uncertain slot is Dragon Ball versus Love-Potion/Ranma/Detective.
